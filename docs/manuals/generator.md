## 1. Overview

The Generator is a synchronous AC machine directly coupled to Steam Turbine 01. It converts the mechanical energy from the turbine into electrical power for the grid. The generator operates at 3000 rpm to produce 50 Hz AC power. It is cooled by the Cooling System and monitored for winding temperature, output voltage, and output current.

### Key Specifications

|-----------|-------|
| Type | Synchronous, 2-pole, horizontal |

## 2. Principle of Operation

### 2.1 Startup and Synchronization

1. Verify Cooling System is operational — coolant flowing through heat exchangers.
2. Confirm the excitation system is ready (static exciter or brushless exciter).
3. Steam Turbine 01 brings the Generator to near-rated speed (approx. 2950 rpm).
4. Apply excitation to bring terminal voltage to rated value (11 kV).
5. The automatic synchronizer matches:
   - Voltage magnitude (generator = grid)
   - Frequency (generator = grid)
   - Phase angle (within ±10°)
6. Close the generator circuit breaker to connect to the grid.
7. Gradually increase load by increasing steam flow to the turbine and excitation current.

### 2.2 Normal Operation

- **Winding Temperature** must remain below 115°C. Above 105°C triggers alarm; above 115°C requires load reduction.
- **Output Voltage** should be regulated at 11 kV ± 5% by the automatic voltage regulator (AVR).
- **Output Current** should not exceed 550 A continuously. Brief overloads up to 600 A are permitted per the machine thermal capability curve.
- Monitor bearing temperatures (shared with Steam Turbine 01 — same shaft line).
- Check for abnormal sounds — humming, sparking (if brush-type exciter), or grinding.

### 2.3 Shutdown Procedure

1. Reduce active power output to minimum by reducing steam flow.
2. Reduce reactive power to zero by reducing excitation.
3. Open the generator circuit breaker.
4. Remove excitation.
5. Coast down with Steam Turbine 01.

## 3. System Functions

### 3.1 Electrical Generation

The rotor (field winding) carries DC excitation current, creating a rotating magnetic field. As the rotor spins at 3000 rpm, the magnetic field sweeps past the stator (armature) windings, inducing an AC voltage by Faraday's law. The output frequency is determined by rotor speed (f = RPM × poles / 120). The output voltage is controlled by adjusting excitation current.

### 3.2 Excitation System

The excitation system supplies DC current to the rotor field winding. In a static exciter configuration, a thyristor bridge rectifies power from the generator terminals and feeds it to the rotor via slip rings and brushes. The AVR modulates the thyristor firing angle to maintain constant terminal voltage.

### 3.3 Cooling System Interface

The Generator's heat exchangers receive coolant from the Cooling System. Heat generated by I²R losses in the windings, core losses, and friction is transferred to the coolant. The Cooling System dissipates this heat via cooling towers or air-cooled heat exchangers.

### 3.4 Protection System

The Generator is protected by a comprehensive relay system including:
- Differential protection (internal faults)
- Overcurrent protection (external faults and backup)
- Stator earth fault protection
- Rotor earth fault protection
- Reverse power protection (motoring)
- Loss of excitation protection
- Overvoltage / undervoltage protection
- Winding temperature protection

## 4. Failure Symptoms and Diagnostics

### 4.1 Overheating (Related Down Event: Generator Overheat)

**Symptoms:**
- Winding Temperature sensor reading above 105°C (alarm) or 115°C (trip)
- Distinctive hot electrical smell (varnish or insulation)
- Output Current readings higher than expected for given load
- Cooling System coolant temperature higher than normal
- Reduced generator efficiency

**Root Causes:**
- Thermal Fatigue — insulation degradation reducing heat dissipation
- Cooling System failure — coolant flow reduced or temperature elevated
- Electrical Fault — inter-turn short circuit causing localized heating
- Overloading beyond rated capacity
- Blocked ventilation paths or fouled heat exchangers

**Recommended Actions:**
1. If Winding Temperature exceeds 115°C, reduce load immediately by 25%.
2. If temperature does not stabilize, trip the generator and turbine.
3. Check Cooling System — verify coolant flow rate and inlet temperature.
4. Inspect heat exchangers for fouling — clean if necessary.
5. Perform insulation resistance test (megger test) on stator windings:
   - Minimum acceptable: 100 MΩ at 40°C (PI > 2.0).
6. If insulation is degraded, schedule winding inspection and possible rewinding.
7. Check for restricted ventilation — inspect air filters and ducting.

**Related Causes:** Thermal Fatigue, Electrical Fault
**Required Competences:** Electrical Systems (Advanced)
**Responsible Roles:** Electrician

---

### 4.2 Insulation Degradation

**Symptoms:**
- Declining megger test results over successive outages
- Partial discharge activity detected during online monitoring
- Elevated Winding Temperature for given load (reduced thermal conductivity)
- Intermittent earth fault alarms
- Visible discoloration or tracking marks on winding end-turns during inspection

**Root Causes:**
- Thermal aging — cumulative thermal stress exceeding insulation class F limits
- Moisture ingress during outages
- Electrical surge damage from switching or lightning
- Oil contamination from bearing lubrication system

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Perform polarization index (PI) and dielectric absorption ratio tests.
2. Conduct partial discharge mapping to locate defects.
3. If PI < 2.0 or PD activity excessive, plan generator inspection.
4. Dry out windings if moisture suspected (circulate warm dry air).
5. Clean windings with approved solvents if contamination found.
6. If insulation failure confirmed, schedule rewind by qualified service shop.

**Responsible Roles:** Electrician, Maintenance Planner

---

### 4.3 Vibration and Alignment Issues

**Symptoms:**
- Vibration on Generator bearings higher than turbine bearings
- 2x running speed frequency dominant (misalignment signature)
- Bearing temperature elevated on one end only
- Visible shaft orbit deviation from normal centerline

**Root Causes:**
- Misalignment between Generator and Steam Turbine 01 coupling
- Foundation settlement or looseness
- Thermal growth mismatch
- Rotor electrical unbalance (shorted turns in field winding)

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Record vibration spectrum to distinguish mechanical from electrical causes.
2. Perform coupling alignment check using dial indicators or laser alignment.
3. Correct alignment to within OEM specifications (typically 0.05mm offset, 0.05mm/100mm angularity).
4. If electrical unbalance suspected, perform field winding impedance test.
5. Check foundation bolts for looseness.

**Required Competences:** Vibration Analysis (Expert), Turbine Operation (Advanced)
**Responsible Roles:** Turbine Engineer, Senior Mechanic

### 4.4 General Failure Symptoms

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Initial Check |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Loss of excitation | AVR failure, slip ring wear, field breaker trip | Check AVR status, inspect slip rings/brushes |
| Reverse power | Steam supply loss, check valve closed | Verify turbine steam flow, check valves |
| Stator earth fault | Insulation failure, moisture ingress | Megger test, locate fault with bridging method |
| Output voltage oscillation | AVR tuning, grid disturbance | Check AVR settings, grid frequency |
| Bearing sparks (brush-type) | Brush wear, contamination, spring tension | Inspect brush length, clean slip rings |

## 5. Preventive Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Visual inspection | Daily | Check for abnormal sounds, odors, readings |
| Winding Temperature monitoring | Continuous | Trend data, compare to load baseline |
| Brush inspection (if applicable) | Monthly | Check length, spring tension, sparking |
| Insulation resistance test | Quarterly | Megger test at 5 kV DC for 11 kV machine |
| Cooling system heat exchanger clean | Quarterly | Backwash or chemically clean as needed |
| Thermographic survey | Quarterly | IR scan of connections, terminals, bus work |
| Generator winding test | Yearly | Full electrical test suite (IR, PI, PD, impedance) |
| Major inspection | 5 years | Partial or full disassembly, winding condition assessment |

## 6. Safety Precautions

- **LETHAL VOLTAGE** — 11 kV is present during operation. Only qualified electricians may work on generator electrical systems.
- Always ground the generator terminals and rotor before any maintenance (stored charge hazard).
- LOTO on the generator circuit breaker and excitation system before any work.
- Do not open generator inspection covers while the rotor is spinning.
- Hydrogen-cooled generators (if applicable) require special safety procedures for explosive atmospheres.
- Wear PPE: arc-rated clothing, safety glasses, insulated gloves for electrical work.
- The Generator room requires restricted access — authorized personnel only.

## 1. Overview

The Combustion Chamber is the enclosed furnace space within Boiler Unit 01 where fuel combustion takes place. It houses the burner assembly, refractory lining, and flame detection systems. Heat generated by combustion is transferred to the boiler water tubes that form the chamber walls. Proper combustion management is essential for boiler efficiency, emissions compliance, and safety.

### Key Specifications

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Design Temperature | 1400°C (flame zone) |
| Normal Flame Temperature | 1000-1400°C |

## 2. Principle of Operation

### 2.1 Combustion Process

The burner mixes fuel (natural gas) with combustion air in controlled ratios. The ideal stoichiometric ratio produces approximately 0% excess O2, but practical operation requires 3-5% excess O2 for complete combustion and safety margin. The flame is anchored to the burner quarl (refractory tile) and fills the combustion chamber volume. Heat radiates from the flame to the water tubes lining the walls.

### 2.2 Startup Sequence

1. Confirm Boiler Unit 01 feed water system is ready (water level above 40%).
2. Verify combustion air damper is in purge position (full open).
3. Initiate furnace purge — 5 air volume changes at full airflow rate.
4. Verify Flame Temperature sensor is reading ambient (no residual flame).
5. Energize spark igniter and open pilot gas valve.
6. Confirm pilot flame detected by flame scanner within the timeout period (10 seconds).
7. Open main fuel valve gradually — main flame establishes.
8. Modulate fuel and air ratio to achieve target Exhaust Gas O2 of 3-5%.
9. Ramp firing rate according to Boiler Unit 01 warm-up curve.

### 2.3 Normal Operation

- **Flame Temperature** should be stable between 1000-1400°C. Sudden drops indicate flame instability or fuel interruption.
- **Exhaust Gas O2** is the primary combustion efficiency indicator:
  - Below 3%: fuel-rich, incomplete combustion, risk of CO production and soot.
  - 3-5%: optimum efficiency with complete combustion.
  - Above 5%: excess air, heat wasted to stack, reduced efficiency.
- Observe flame through sight glass — should be blue with well-defined shape, no flickering or lifting.
- No unusual vibrations, rumbling, or pulsations.

### 2.4 Shutdown Procedure

1. Close main fuel valve (automatic on BMS trip or manual command).
2. Purge furnace with air for 5 minutes to remove residual fuel.
3. Close combustion air damper after purge complete.
4. Confirm Flame Temperature sensor reads below 100°C before allowing access.

## 3. System Functions

### 3.1 Burner Management System (BMS)

The BMS is a safety-rated controller that manages the startup, monitoring, and shutdown of the combustion process. It continuously monitors flame scanner output and will trip the fuel valves within 4 seconds if flame is lost. The BMS also enforces the pre-ignition purge and post-shutdown purge sequences.

### 3.2 Air-Fuel Ratio Control

Combustion efficiency depends on the correct ratio of air to fuel. The control system modulates the air damper and fuel valve in a coordinated sequence (cross-limited control) to ensure air always leads fuel on increasing load and fuel always leads air on decreasing load. This prevents fuel-rich conditions that could cause an explosion.

### 3.3 Refractory Lining

The chamber walls are lined with high-alumina refractory material that insulates the steel casing from direct flame exposure and reflects radiant heat back toward the water tubes. Refractory condition must be monitored during outages — spalling or cracking exposes the steel casing to overheating.

## 4. Failure Symptoms and Diagnostics

### 4.1 Flame Failure (Related Down Event: Combustion Chamber Flame Failure)

**Symptoms:**
- Flame scanner signal lost — BMS trips fuel valves
- Flame Temperature drops rapidly to ambient
- Boiler Unit 01 pressure begins falling (no heat input)
- Alarm annunciates: "FLAME FAILURE TRIP"
- Exhaust Gas O2 reading rises to 21% (pure air, no combustion)

**Root Causes:**
- Foreign Object Damage — debris blocking burner nozzle or gas injection ports
- Fuel supply interruption (gas pressure drop or valve malfunction)
- Flame scanner lens fouled (false trip — no actual flame loss)
- Combustion air interruption (fan trip)
- Refractory spalling disrupting flame stability

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Do **NOT** attempt immediate restart. Allow BMS to complete post-trip purge.
2. Check flame scanner lens — clean if dirty or coated with soot.
3. Verify fuel gas supply pressure is within operating range.
4. Inspect burner nozzle for blockage — remove any foreign objects.
5. Check combustion air fan operation and damper position.
6. If all checks pass, attempt one restart. If flame fails again, lock out for investigation.
7. Inspect refractory lining for damage — repair if spalling or cracking found.

**Related Causes:** Foreign Object Damage
**Responsible Roles:** Boiler Operator, Shift Supervisor

---

### 4.2 Poor Combustion / High CO

**Symptoms:**
- Exhaust Gas O2 below 3% (fuel-rich condition)
- Visible smoke from boiler stack (dark or yellow)
- Combustion chamber runs louder than normal (rumbling)
- Carbon monoxide alarm in boiler room
- Fuel consumption higher than expected for steam output

**Root Causes:**
- Burner nozzle wear causing poor fuel atomization
- Combustion air filter fouled or damper sticking
- Improper air-fuel ratio calibration
- Flame impingement on refractory (wrong burner position)

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Increase combustion air slightly to raise O2 above 3%.
2. Schedule burner inspection — clean or replace nozzle.
3. Check air filter and damper actuator for freedom of movement.
4. Recalibrate air-fuel ratio curve using combustion analyzer.
5. Perform combustion efficiency test (CO, CO2, O2 measurement).

**Responsible Roles:** Boiler Operator, Instrument Technician

---

### 4.3 Refractory Damage

**Symptoms:**
- Hot spots on the steel casing (detected by thermal imaging)
- Visible cracks or gaps in refractory during inspection
- Pieces of refractory material found in the furnace bottom
- Increased casing temperature near damaged area
- Reduced combustion efficiency (heat escaping through casing)

**Root Causes:**
- Thermal Fatigue — repeated heating/cooling cycles
- Mechanical impact during maintenance
- Incorrect refractory material or installation
- Overfiring exceeding design temperature

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Mark and photograph damaged areas during outage inspection.
2. Remove loose refractory material back to sound base.
3. Anchor new refractory with stainless steel anchors.
4. Cure new refractory per manufacturer instructions (slow heat-up).
5. Consider upgrade to higher-grade alumina refractory in high-wear areas.

### 4.4 General Failure Symptoms

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Initial Check |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Flame pulsation | Air-fuel imbalance, restricted fuel line | Check gas pressure, air damper response |
| Pilot won't light | Gas supply, igniter, flame scanner | Verify pilot gas pressure, spark visible |
| High stack temperature | Tube fouling, excess air, refractory damage | Check O2 level, inspect tube cleanliness |
| Burner flashback | Gas pressure too low, worn nozzle | Increase gas pressure, inspect nozzle |

## 5. Preventive Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Flame scanner cleaning | Weekly | Wipe lens with soft cloth |
| Combustion analysis | Monthly | Measure O2, CO, CO2 at operating load |
| Burner inspection | Quarterly | Remove and inspect nozzle, check for carbon buildup |
| Refractory visual inspection | Quarterly | Inspect through access doors for cracks, spalling |
| Air filter change | Quarterly | Replace combustion air intake filters |
| Comprehensive refractory survey | Yearly | Enter furnace during outage, full inspection and repair |
| BMS function test | Yearly | Simulate flame failure, verify trip response time < 4s |

---

## 6. Safety Precautions

- **NEVER** bypass the flame scanner or BMS safety interlocks.
- **NEVER** attempt to relight the burner without completing a full furnace purge.
- Gas detectors must be functional in the boiler room at all times.
- Allow adequate cool-down (below 50°C) before entering the combustion chamber.
- Use LOTO on fuel valves before entering the furnace.
- Wear PPE: safety glasses, heat-resistant clothing, hard hat, gas monitor.
- Confined space entry procedures apply for all furnace access.
